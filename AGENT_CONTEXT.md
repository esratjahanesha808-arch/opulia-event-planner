# PART 1 — BUSINESS STRATEGY

## 1.1 What Opulia Is
Opulia is a **premium event planning and design studio**. It is NOT a wedding-only company and must never be presented as one. It plans and designs:

- Signature Weddings
- Haldi Ceremonies
- Mehendi Ceremonies
- Nikkah Ceremonies
- Birthdays (all scales)
- Anniversaries
- Destination Events (cross-border)
- Venue Decoration & Styling
- Private & Custom Events

**Positioning:** Opulia is positioned above the market average. It does not compete on price. It competes on design quality, execution precision, cultural sensitivity, and the feeling of being completely cared for.

---

## 1.2 Target Customer Groups

| Event Type | Primary Customer | Secondary Customer |
|---|---|---|
| Weddings | Couples aged 24–40, professionals, families with budget | Parents paying for children's weddings |
| Haldi/Mehendi/Nikkah | South Asian families who want cultural authenticity + modern aesthetics | Diaspora families based abroad |
| Birthdays/Anniversaries | High-income individuals wanting milestone events | Corporate gifting / partner surprises |
| Destination Events | Couples/families wanting abroad events with zero local knowledge | NRIs wanting events in their home country |
| Custom Events | Anyone with an unusual, uncategorized vision | Brands wanting private brand events |

**Core customer insight:** The ideal Opulia client is **not price-sensitive by nature** — they are *quality-sensitive*. They will spend freely IF they trust the planner completely. Trust, not price, is the primary purchase trigger.

---

## 1.3 Customer Psychology — What Makes Them Book

### What they fear (the real objections):
1. **"What if something goes wrong on the day?"** → Address with: Process section (step-by-step) + testimonials that explicitly mention smooth execution
2. **"Will it feel generic?"** → Address with: Bespoke language, portfolio variety, service descriptions that emphasize personalization
3. **"Will they understand our culture/preferences?"** → Address with: Cultural services explicitly named (Haldi, Mehendi, Nikkah), cultural language in copy
4. **"Is this worth the price?"** → Address with: Before showing packages, show the experience, the portfolio, and the testimonials. Justify the value before revealing the investment.
5. **"Am I the right kind of client for them?"** → Address with: Showing multiple event types and scales — not just grand weddings

### What builds instant trust:
- Real photography and video (not stock illustrations)
- Named testimonials with specific events and locations
- A clearly explained process (shows professionalism)
- Confident, specific language (not vague luxury-speak)
- Visible cultural awareness (naming events like Haldi, Mehendi, Nikkah correctly)
- An inquiry form that asks intelligent questions (shows they're serious professionals)

### What destroys trust instantly:
- Generic stock photos of brides in western settings
- Fake statistics with no context
- Vague copy: "we make your dreams come true"
- Prices shown before value is established
- A contact form that asks only name + email + message
- Any visual that looks like a template

---

## 1.4 The Sales Journey (Customer Flow)

The website is designed to move the visitor through these emotional and rational states in order. **Do not break this sequence.**

```
DISCOVER
  ↓ (Hero video — instant visual impact. No words needed yet.)
UNDERSTAND
  ↓ (Services — "Oh, they do my type of event.")
IMAGINE
  ↓ (Services + Portfolio — "I can picture my event like that.")
TRUST
  ↓ (Process + Testimonials — "They are professional. Others loved them.")
JUSTIFY
  ↓ (Packages — "I can see what the investment looks like.")
CONNECT
  ↓ (About — "These feel like real people I'd want to work with.")
ACT
  ↓ (CTA Section + Footer — "I'm ready to talk to them.")
```

**Critical rule:** The Packages section (investment/pricing) must ALWAYS come AFTER the Services and Process sections. Never show the price before the value is established.

# PART 3 — CONTENT STRATEGY

## 3.1 Brand Voice

| Dimension | Direction |
|---|---|
| Tone | Confident, calm, elegant — not boastful |
| Personality | Expert advisor, not salesperson |
| Language level | Intelligent adult, not corporate jargon |
| Warmth | Present but understated — warmth through specificity, not through adjectives |
| Cultural awareness | Fluent — names events correctly, understands the emotional weight of celebrations |

## 3.2 Writing Rules (MANDATORY for all content)

### DO:
- Use specific language: "From venue selection and floral direction to day-of coordination" NOT "We handle everything"
- Name real event types: "Haldi, Mehendi, Nikkah" NOT "cultural events"
- Lead with the customer's experience: "You deserve to walk into your own event as a guest — fully present" NOT "We provide excellent service"
- Use contrast for effect: "We don't coordinate events — we design atmospheres."
- Let testimonials be specific: mention the type of event, the city, a concrete moment
- Headlines should promise an outcome or feeling, not describe a feature

### DO NOT:
- Use: "Making your dreams come true"
- Use: "Where dreams become reality"
- Use: "Creating unforgettable memories"
- Use: "We do it all"
- Use: "One-stop shop"
- Use: "Your big day"
- Use vague luxury filler: "Premium", "World-class", "Top-tier" without context
- Write copy that could apply to any event company
- Show prices before showing the portfolio and testimonials

## 3.3 Section-by-Section Content Guidance

### Hero (Timed — 4 phases × 4 seconds)
Cinematic, minimalist luxury hero: full-screen video with timed high-contrast editorial typography.
No cluttering badges, secondary buttons, progress bars, or slide counters.
Each phase delivers:
- One large, emotionally resonant editorial headline (2 lines max)
- One grounding, clean geometric sans supporting line

**Phase 1 (Weddings):**
- Headline: "Where every vow finds its perfect setting."
- Sub: "Full-service wedding planning & design — from first concept to final farewell."

**Phase 2 (Cultural):**
- Headline: "Culture honoured. Beauty elevated."
- Sub: "We celebrate every tradition with the artistry and care it deserves."

**Phase 3 (Milestones):**
- Headline: "Grand moments for people who deserve it most."
- Sub: "From intimate gatherings to extraordinary celebrations — every scale, curated."

**Phase 4 (All events):**
- Headline: "We plan it all. So you can feel it all."
- Sub: "Destination events, anniversaries, private events — one team, unlimited capability."

- Section label: "What We Do"
- Header title: "Every Occasion, Crafted with Intention"
- Right header subtext: Focus on the customer benefit (they can be present), not the feature list
- Each card: number → name → 2–3 sentence description that speaks to the specific customer need → "Discover →" link
- Hover state: card goes dark green, name turns gold — signals depth behind each service

### Packages Section
- Section label: "Investment"

- Signature = "Most Popular" badge
- Each package name uses italic Bodoni Moda: "The Curation" / "The Full Story" / "The Exclusive"

### Process Section

- Header: "A Seamless Journey from Idea to Reality"
- Lead copy: Centre on the customer being present, not stressed, at their own event
- 4 steps: The Conversation → The Design → The Logistics → The Day
- Each step: number tag → bold title → 1–2 sentence explanation
- The word "invisible" in Step 4 is intentional — it signals the planner disappears into the background

### Portfolio / Past Events

- Subtext (right): Keep it brief, honest — "Client videos featured here. Each tile is a real celebration."

### Testimonials

- Each card structure: avatar → name + event type → quote → star rating
- **Quotes must feel human.** They should mention: a specific moment, a feeling, a concrete outcome (not just "they were great")

### About Section

# PART 4 — VISUAL SYSTEM (APPROVED — DO NOT CHANGE WITHOUT CLIENT APPROVAL)

## 4.1 Color System

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Deep Green | `#17230f` | Primary dark surface, nav scrolled, footer, dark sections |
| Green Light | `#243318` | Hover states on green backgrounds |
| Alabaster White | `#F9F8F6` | Primary light surface, light sections |
| Off White | `#F0EFEA` | Subtle variant for hover states on white |
| Gold | `#c8a96e` | Section labels, progress bars, star ratings, pkg badges, dividers only |
| Gold Dim | `rgba(200,169,110,0.3)` | Subtle borders on dark sections |
| Text on Green | `rgba(249,248,246,0.68)` | Body copy on dark green backgrounds |

**Strict alternating section pattern:**
- Odd sections (1,3,5,7,9): `#F9F8F6` white
- Even sections (2,4,6,8): `#17230f` green
(Hero is always dark green overlay regardless)

## 4.2 Typography
 
| Role | Font | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wordmark (OPULIA) | Plus Jakarta Sans | 400 | 8px letter-spacing, centred in nav |
| Headings (H1–H4) | Bodoni Moda | 500 (opsz auto) | High-contrast, editorial serif display font |
| Subheadings / Body / UI | Plus Jakarta Sans | 300–600 | Clean, geometric sans-serif font |

## 4.3 Spacing Philosophy
- Section padding: `8rem 6%` (desktop), `5rem 5%` (mobile)
- Max content width: `1440px`, always centred
- Cards and grids: use gap not margin for internal spacing
- Breathing room between elements is luxury — never crowd

## 4.4 Button System

| Class | Background | Border | Text | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `.btn-white` | White | White | Green | Primary CTA on dark sections |
| `.btn-outline-white` | Transparent | White 50% | White | Secondary CTA on dark sections |
| `.btn-green` | Green | Green | White | Primary CTA on light sections |
| `.btn-outline-green` | Transparent | Green | Green | Secondary CTA on light sections |

- All buttons: `border-radius: 0` (sharp), uppercase, `letter-spacing: 2.5px`, `font-size: 0.75rem`
- Hover: subtle `translateY(-2px)` lift + colour inversion

## 4.5 Animation Philosophy
- "Slow luxury" — nothing snaps or bounces
- Reveal: `opacity 0 → 1` + `translateY(36px → 0)` over `0.95s` with `cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)`
- Hero text transitions: `opacity + translateY(-16px)` over `600ms`
- Progress bars: `width 0→100%` in `4000ms linear`
- Hover transitions: `0.3s–0.4s ease`
- No bounce, no elastic, no flash

## 7.1 The Single Most Important Rule

## 7.2 CSS Block Management — CRITICAL
**When editing `style.css` using node scripts, ALWAYS check for and avoid deleting CSS blocks that belong to OTHER sections.**

**What went wrong (Oct 2026):**
A script that rebuilt the Portfolio CSS accidentally wiped the `home-process-*` CSS block (the sticky horizontal timeline for the Process section). This caused the entire "How We Work" section to lose all styles.

**Rules to prevent this:**
- Before writing a CSS block, always log ALL section boundaries first (`/* === ... */` comments) to understand what is at each position in the file.
- When inserting new CSS, use `.indexOf()` on a UNIQUE section identifier string (not just `PORTFOLIO`) to find the exact insertion point.
- When replacing a CSS block, replace ONLY the start-to-end of that specific section, never a range that could overlap another section.
- After any CSS edit, verify the file still contains all expected section keywords (`home-process-section`, `home-process-sticky`, `portfolio-section`, `testi-section`, etc.)

## 7.3 Process Section — Technical Details (DO NOT BREAK)
The Process ("How We Work") section uses a sticky horizontal scroll timeline:
- HTML class: `.home-process-section` (NOT `.process-section`)
